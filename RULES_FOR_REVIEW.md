# Shipment enquiry scoring rules for review

16 November 2026

Bahaa agreed on shipment identity, checking the records available when the agent replied, escalation timing, and qualifying repeat contacts on 16 November 2026. He also chose to grade discrepancies in the context of the enquiry, record failures in the initial handling after any recovery, and report the outcome, severity and measured difference separately. The service deadlines are prototype conventions for discussion with the pilot workflow owner. ## The eight original questions

| Question | Recommended rule |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Which time boundaries count? | Escalate a pre-arrival delay when the latest recorded ETA exceeds the original planned arrival for the same milestone by **more than 24 hours**. Measure **2 hours** to escalate, **48 hours** from qualifying case opening to actual customer update, and **7 days** from original receipt to a qualifying repeat. Those three limits include their exact boundaries. Use elapsed time. For ETA accuracy, use the recorded estimate as described below. |
| 2. What if the customer quotes another booking they own? | Grade against the authorized shipment the customer unambiguously identified using information available at response time. Use a confirmed correction once recorded. Keep the customer's simulated private intent separate from the observable score. |
| 3. Can a missing ETA be recovered? | Use the actual sent message when it records a clear claim. A run log may supply the missing claim if it identifies a unique matching sent response, with consistent enquiry, shipment and timing. Tracking establishes the reference ETA. An agent's success label alone supplies no claim. Mark the ETA check `unknown` if the transmitted claim cannot be verified. |
| 4. What if escalation was unnecessary? | Record the unnecessary action and include its measured work and cost. Preserve a verified resolution when the answer and all required actions meet the rules. Flag a policy violation separately if the workflow explicitly prohibits the action. |
| 5. What if a person rescues the case after the deadline? | Record the missed deadline and how late the action was when the evidence supports the timing. Preserve the original failure, later correction, eventual outcome, and human effort where measured. A late case can end in a documented resolution. |
| 6. Does a repeat contact always count against the first answer? | Treat same customer and shipment inside seven days as a signal to check. Count a qualifying repeat when the record links it to the same unresolved issue, for example a follow-up on the original unanswered ticket. A substantive new ETA or other new issue starts a new enquiry when the evidence supports that distinction. Leave an unresolvable linkage `unknown`. |
| 7. What if a qualifying delay appears while the enquiry is waiting? | Use the recorded ETA available when the reply was sent to judge answer accuracy. Start the two-hour escalation clock at receipt if the qualifying delay is already known then. If it first becomes known while the enquiry is being handled, start the clock at the recording time of that first qualifying update. The delay must concern the requested milestone. |
| 8. How do we score an escalated case that is still open? | Record `escalated_open` when the linked case is demonstrably still open at the export cutoff. Keep it in eligible enquiry volume and score timeliness separately. Distinguish an open case from one whose records cannot establish its state. |

## ETA accuracy for the prototype

Compare the ETA in the reply to the latest authoritative ETA available when the agent replied for the requested shipment, port and milestone. For structured timestamps, convert both values to UTC and record the reply ETA minus the reference ETA. Zero records an exact match. A positive difference means the reply gave a later ETA. A negative difference means it gave an earlier ETA. For a date-only source or request, compare the local dates at the supported precision. A missing reference ETA or an unverified time zone can leave the difference unknown. Report arrival as actual once recorded and label a planned time as planned if it is the only schedule information available.

Maersk publishes revised ETA by leg and CMA CGM provides ETA for the port of discharge. MSC describes updates when estimates change sufficiently. CMA CGM's four working hours in its U.S. onboarding guide is a support response target. Hapag-Lloyd's one-day window measures vessel schedule reliability. These sources establish how carriers communicate and report timing and leave the tolerance for the ETA quoted in a reply unspecified. For our evaluator we use the carrier record itself. [Maersk transport plan notice](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/07/08/transport-plan-change-notice-downloadable-document) · [CMA CGM tracking](https://www.cma-cgm.com/products-services/ecommerce/container-tracking) · [CMA CGM onboarding guide, p. 23](https://www.cma-cgm.com/assets/public/documents/CS%20Digital%20Onboarding%20Kit_1.pdf?VersionId=qwCQ.zIZm3gjRoWFZS2Qfb75LP3IgK0E) · [MSC Track & Trace](https://developerportal.msc.com/content/TnT_Subscription_Customer_Onboarding.pdf) · [Hapag-Lloyd quality promises](https://www.hapag-lloyd.com/en/online-business/quality-promises-solution/about-quality-promises.html)

For forecast analysis, record the signed difference between the ETA available before arrival and the eventual actual arrival for the same milestone. An early error and a late error can have different costs. Choosing different penalties requires evidence about those costs. [Point forecast evaluation](https://www.bundesbank.de/resource/blob/635562/7d3de0f3fc003e5b4864828143f268cf/mL/2012-06-01-eltville-11-gneiting-paper-data.pdf)

The ETA reference gives a measured difference. The grade depends on what the customer asked and what the discrepancy could change. Earlier fixtures and bulk exports retain their versioned scoring rules for reproducibility.

## Grade the handling in context

Report the **verified service outcome**, the **severity and reason for each scored finding**, and the **measured difference or delay**. Keep the result of each required check and the evidence behind it. The synthetic examples below define the first interpretations for reviewer calibration.

| Severity | When it applies |
| --- | --- |
| None | The evidence supports the answer or action at the precision relevant to the request. |
| Minor | The discrepancy leaves the requested information or decision substantially intact. In a routine tracking enquiry, an 11:01 reply for a recorded 11:00 ETA is a one-minute discrepancy. The enquiry can be resolved if the reply otherwise answers the request. |
| Material | The discrepancy changes the answer to a decision the customer asked about. For a time-sensitive enquiry, check whether it changes the conclusion about a documented cutoff for the requested milestone. Use confirmed availability or appointment records to assess a pickup decision. |
| Critical | The evidence establishes a serious failure of a required check, such as answering about the wrong container or omitting a mandatory escalation once it was due. Flag the initial failure and record any verified correction and eventual outcome. |
| Unknown | The transcript, authoritative reference or relevant context is insufficient to establish the severity. Keep any verifiable facts and the service outcome where independently supported. |

If an escalation due in two hours occurs one second after the deadline, record a missed deadline and one second of lateness. Grade the timing in context and report any eventual verified resolution. A mandatory escalation that was never completed warrants its own finding when the available follow-up establishes the omission. Record a documented change to a customer decision separately from a potential consequence.

## Judge outcomes from the case history

The 14-day period was a shared observation limit for the earlier synthetic tests. Use the recorded events and the actual export cutoff to determine what can be scored. The original cases retain their versioned expectations. The proposed `0.3.0-proposed` fixtures use an explicit cutoff for each scenario.

- **A status-only enquiry** can be resolved when the verified reply meets the request and any required action is complete. Keep the repeat check pending until the separate seven-day observation window is covered, where needed. Reassess the enquiry if a later record establishes a qualifying repeat or reopening.
- **An escalated enquiry** is resolved when the records show a valid closure reason and the required actions were completed. Record the actual resolution time and score the escalation and customer update deadlines independently.
- **A terminal closure without resolution**, such as `customer_unreachable`, is `closed_unresolved`. **A verified open case** remains open as of the last export. **Missing or conflicting evidence**, such as a closed ticket with no reason or outcome record, can leave the outcome `unknown`. A documented reopening updates the recorded status as of the relevant cutoff. Trace duplicate or merged cases to the original when the link is recorded and count the outcome once. [Maersk case management](https://www.maersk.com/insights/digitalisation/2024/12/23/contacting-customer-service) · [CMA CGM case status](https://www.cma-cgm.com/local/canada/news/410/canada-my-customer-service-mycs-platform)

S23 and S24 show the same escalation at two export cutoffs, while open and after a verified closure more than 14 days after intake. S25 records a terminal closure because the customer was unreachable. S26 records the closure with no reason or outcome evidence.

For group comparisons, define a comparable follow-up checkpoint in advance and use the same elapsed age and equivalent record coverage for both groups. Report counts for resolved, closed unresolved, open and unknown enquiries. Record the case's actual elapsed time when it resolves. Cases still open at the end of observation have an unknown eventual resolution time. The checkpoint serves the comparison and is separate from the service deadlines. [NIST on incomplete follow-up](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/apr/section1/apr131.htm)

## Verify the shipment and milestone

- **Shipment identity and clarification.** A missing, ambiguous or conflicting reference calls for a recorded clarification request. Use `awaiting_customer` if we can verify the clarification was sent and the customer has yet to provide a confirmed reference by the cutoff. A documented confirmation within the same enquiry lets us evaluate the subsequent answer against that shipment. Link the confirmation and reply to the original enquiry. Carriers support lookup by booking or transport document and by container. [Maersk tracking guide](https://www.maersk.com/support/faqs/how-to-track-shipments) · [CMA CGM tracking guide](https://www.cma-cgm.com/ECommerce/ContainerTracking)
- **Container, port and milestone.** Match the reply to the requested container and milestone before comparing times or statuses. Two containers under one booking can have different recorded events. An actual arrival at a transshipment port establishes arrival at that port. A claim of arrival at the final destination needs evidence for the final port. Use the most recent applicable record that was available when the reply was sent. [Maersk shipment details](https://www.maersk.com/support/faqs/how-to-manage-shipments) · [Maersk transport plan changes](https://www.maersk.com/news/articles/2026/07/08/transport-plan-change-notice-downloadable-document) · [CMA CGM tracking notifications](https://www.cma-cgm.com/assets/public/pdf/Notifications%20Centre%20-CMA%20CGM.pdf)

## Measure what AI support adds to the team

Record who performed each verified step and what the team achieved. The AI may retrieve shipment data, answer a routine enquiry or route an exception. Staff may investigate, review, correct or follow up. Ticket opening and closing times measure elapsed time. That interval can include waiting, breaks and work on other cases. Use active-work logs only after checking how their timers behave. If reliable logs are unavailable, use a representative sample to estimate effort by task and report its uncertainty. Where neither is available, mark active effort as `unknown` even when the actions and resolution are verified. Count linked work once and avoid double-counting overlapping time. [Zendesk on handling and resolution time](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/7773692416666-Why-is-my-Ticket-handling-time-value-lower-than-the-Full-resolution-time-of-a-ticket)

Calculate the employer's labour cost per paid workday by dividing monthly employment cost, including salary and applicable employer-paid benefits and contributions, by paid workdays in the same period. Estimate practical hours available for the workflow separately, allowing for breaks, meetings, training and other assigned duties. Use matching cost and capacity estimates for the same staff and activities to calculate an hourly rate. Keep the pay and workload assumptions visible at team level. [Kaplan and Anderson on costing practical capacity](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=04-045.pdf)

Use a phased rollout or another credible team comparison with similar case mix and equivalent follow-up. Check verified resolutions, response quality, repeat contacts, human effort and operating costs. Include AI integration, usage and maintenance costs. Estimate capacity freed after accounting for staff time spent on reviews and corrections. Then check where the team used the capacity and what the additional work achieved, such as more complex exceptions resolved or fewer customers waiting for updates. Track employee outcomes where evidence is available, such as reduced overtime and more time for training. [World Bank on causal comparisons and randomized assignment](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/sief-trust-fund/publication/impact-evaluation-in-practice)

Record cash savings only when spending actually falls, for example through lower paid overtime under comparable conditions. Regular salaries can remain fully paid while the same team delivers more. Keep estimated capacity and observed results from reassigning that time visible without adding their values twice. A raise is a possible decision about how to share the gains. The evaluator uses team outcomes and records any implemented pay change separately as an employee benefit and employer cost.
